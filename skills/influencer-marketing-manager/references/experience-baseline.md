# Experienced Operations Baseline

Use this reference as a practical starting prior when a project has not supplied a mature method. It captures patterns repeatedly used by an experienced internal influencer-marketing operations team. The user's objective and active project rules always refine or replace the baseline; mark a baseline choice as an assumption when it has not been validated for the current market, platform, or product.

## How to apply the baseline

Use this precedence:

```text
user objective and constraints
→ active project rules and approved brief
→ this experienced baseline
→ general industry practice
```

When important project inputs are missing, choose a small reversible test, state the baseline assumption, and define the observation that will confirm or revise it. Let the objective, platform, market, and observed supply determine batch size and lane mix; until those inputs are known, use a small illustrative sample rather than a fixed matrix. A baseline is a useful prior, not a substitute for current evidence.

## Creator discovery defaults

These defaults are a sensible first pass for a creator whose recent activity and content quality matter:

- Review activity in the most recent **90 days**; prefer a pattern of continued activity in the most recent **60 days**.
- Examine at least **3–5 representative pieces of content** before a high-confidence selection.
- Separate long-form videos, Shorts, live replays, and other formats when the proposed cooperation depends on format-specific performance.
- Use recent comparable content and its median or typical range. Explain a single breakout rather than letting it represent the channel.
- Check the actual content scene, audience, market, language, brand-safety context, and prior cooperation signals.
- Assign one primary creator lane for outreach; use secondary labels only to retain useful context.
- Use follower count and platform averages as planning signals. Let goal-specific fit, recent evidence, and execution likelihood determine priority.
- Keep a strong content match in the candidate pool when a public business contact is still missing; contactability is a separate readiness result.
- Expand supply only after the current hypothesis and deduplication have been checked. A smaller qualified set is more useful than a full quota of weak matches.

An earlier operating case used fewer than roughly 1,000 views on recent representative long-form videos as a reason to lower priority for one campaign. Treat that figure as a calibration example for discussion, never as a universal threshold. If a project has no threshold, establish one with a small sample and current objectives.

## Example creator lanes

These lanes provide vocabulary for a first hypothesis. The active project may rename, merge, or replace them.

| Lane | Natural content connection | Useful evidence to inspect | Outreach angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tech / 3C** | Device ecosystems, reviews, setup, charging, measurable use | Recent product tests, explanations, long-form consistency, technical accuracy | New device cycle, real workflow, parameters, portability, or desk setup |
| **Outdoor** | Hiking, camping, road trips, fishing, photography, off-grid use | Actual activity and gear use, environment, trip format, audience relevance | Reliable power or utility in a real activity and a concrete scenario |
| **Student / campus** | Dorm, campus, library, commuting, study devices | Authentic student context, audience age/region, practical routines | Everyday study or commuting problem with a simple product use case |
| **Seasonal gifting** | Gift guides, holiday or sale lists, unboxing, purchase recommendations | Clear selection or purchase reasoning, timing, conversion intent | A useful gift choice, reason to buy, and seasonal deadline |
| **Shopping** | Shop-with-me, store visits, hauls, deals, purchase trade-offs | Recent shopping-led long-form content, store context, natural product placement | A current shopping decision, store find, deal, or portable-use moment |

Travel, photography, Apple, desk setup, and similar terms can be secondary scene labels. Choose the lane that best describes the creator's recent main narrative so the message follows the creator's actual context.

## Contact and outreach defaults

Keep **fit** and **readiness** as separate results. For a qualified creator without a ready contact, check the channel's public contact area, linked website or media kit, and clearly associated public profiles in that order. Verify ownership and record the source; a missing address creates a contact task, not a reason to discard the fit.

The first contact should give a truthful selection reason, a real product/use scene, mutual value, a proposed format, and one easy question. Personalize the cooperation story using the creator's primary lane and recent work. Leave price, rights, exclusivity, and delivery promises for the approved commercial conversation. A useful starting cadence is a follow-up after **3–5 business days**, another after **5–7 business days**, and a short close-the-loop note. Before sending, verify the recipient, sender, content, and attachments; after sending, read back the actual state. A substantive human reply starts a tailored-response checkpoint.

## Commercial, fulfillment, and measurement defaults

Treat every human reply as new evidence and re-check fit, performance, authenticity, and risk before presenting it as ready for a commercial decision. Compare the complete package rather than fee alone:

```text
complete cost = fee + rights + exclusivity + tax + logistics + production support + payment cost
estimated CPV = complete cost / expected effective views
```

Use recent comparable content for conservative, base, and optimistic scenarios, and trade scope, rights, exclusivity, timing, revisions, or bundle size deliberately. Turn agreed terms into a checklist with owners, inputs, milestones, publication, disclosure, tracking, payment conditions, and completion evidence. Measure the chosen objective, label attribution limits, and record the next test.

## Promoted experience patterns

These compact patterns are useful for reasoning and for eval cases:

1. **Aggregate data hides format mix.** A channel can look strong overall while recent long-form content is weak because Shorts or old hits lift the average. Split formats and use recent representative medians.
2. **A strong fit can lack an immediate route.** Preserve the candidate, record the missing public contact as readiness work, and re-check identity before sending.
3. **A reply changes the qualification.** Re-read the actual message, extract commercial terms, and re-qualify the creator before a client or contract decision.
4. **The lane changes the proposition.** A shopping creator, outdoor creator, student creator, and technical reviewer need different product scenes and questions even when the product is identical.
5. **Weak response is a learning signal.** Separate delivery, opens, replies, qualified conversations, agreements, and outcomes; test the most plausible cause on a bounded batch before scaling volume.
