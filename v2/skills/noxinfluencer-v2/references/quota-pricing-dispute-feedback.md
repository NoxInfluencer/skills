# Quota, Pricing, Disputes, And Feedback

## Quota And Pricing

Quota is a point-in-time business snapshot. When the user explicitly asks about quota or a credit or entitlement restriction blocks the workflow, report the relevant allowance, restriction, reset or validity information and trusted next action supplied by the service. Routine task updates follow the main Skill's credit-reporting rule. Historical usage supports a requested cost review but does not replace the current quota snapshot.

Pricing is server-owned and can change. Use the current action price when the user asks about cost; do not rely on memorized values. Explain whether cost follows returned creators, exported fields, contacts, messages, reports, or another unit. A successful business action may depend on both Skill Credit and a separate product entitlement.

## Creator Collaboration Disputes

Use a dispute only for a concrete collaboration breach or due-diligence concern about a creator. Read existing records when available before creating another report. Require a specific creator, dispute type, factual description, and concrete screenshot evidence.

Creating, updating, or withdrawing a dispute requires explicit approval. Private reports can hide description and evidence from public views while still exposing type or aggregate count. Do not promise legal resolution, treat allegations as proven facts, or infer a creator verdict solely from one report.

The dispute feature requires paid membership and does not consume Skill Credit. Report an entitlement restriction separately from quota exhaustion so the user receives the correct next action.

## Product Feedback

Use feedback for product behavior, data issues, confusing experience, suggestions, or feature requests. Summarize the category, description, and attachments, then obtain a short confirmation before submission. Feedback does not consume Skill Credit and may receive asynchronous follow-up.

Keep dispute and feedback workflows separate. A creator collaboration breach is not ordinary product feedback, and a product defect is not a creator dispute.
